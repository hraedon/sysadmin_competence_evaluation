This is a false-healthy replication scenario: every agent job reports Success, but the DR replica is stale by 5 days and would fail the business RPO on failover.

The report's own Subscription Status section shows the last successful synchronization was 2026-07-05 03:17, five days before the report date. The Latency column says "5 days". Despite this, the 7-day Latency Trend table shows 0 ms every day because that metric is measuring something different — likely the latency of commands that did get delivered, which has been nothing since 2026-07-05.

The chain of failure is:

1. The network maintenance window on 2026-07-02 caused replication to fall behind.
2. The latency alert auto-cleared on 2026-07-03 when the WAN stabilised, giving the appearance of recovery.
3. On 2026-07-05 the Distribution Agent delivered 1,247,891 commands and then stopped delivering. This may have been the last of the backlog, or the subscriber may have entered a state where it could no longer accept commands.
4. The Distribution clean up job has a 72-hour maximum retention. By 2026-07-08, any undelivered commands older than 72 hours were eligible for deletion. The "Undelivered commands: 0" line confirms the distribution database is now empty, so even if the subscriber reconnects, the gap cannot be filled from the distributor.

The monitoring design is the deeper issue. The organisation only alerts on agent job failure or retry. A job that completes successfully but delivers zero commands is treated as healthy. A job that completes successfully but leaves the subscriber 5 days behind is also treated as healthy. This is inadequate for a 4-hour RPO.

What should be in place:
- Latency alerting based on the age of the most recently applied transaction at the subscriber, not on the latency of delivered commands.
- Validation queries that compare row counts, checksums, or known sentinel rows between publisher and subscriber on a schedule.
- Distribution retention configured to at least 2-3 times the RPO to allow for transient failures and recovery.
- A failover readiness check that blocks failover or warns operators when subscriber lag exceeds the RPO.
- Investigation into why the subscriber stopped applying commands after 2026-07-05, including error logs and agent history with verbose logging.

This would move the environment from job-status monitoring to data-freshness monitoring.
