The headline says "success" and the fine print says "failure" — and the
over-claim is in the metric itself, not in a specific artifact. "100%
locked, 847 tests pass, working well" is the claim. "0% first-attempt, 4.2
mean attempts" is the counter-evidence, and it's in the same artifact. The
calibration: 100% lock rate with 0% first-attempt pass is a failure signal,
not a success signal. The lock rate is the trailing indicator (did it
eventually pass?); the first-attempt pass rate is the leading indicator
(did it produce correct output the first time?). When the trailing is 100%
and the leading is 0%, the pipeline is succeeding by brute force —
retrying until the gates pass — and the retries are masking a quality
problem. "Working well" is the agent reading the headline (100% locked)
and ignoring the fine print (0% first-attempt, 4.2 retries). What I'd
check: what's failing on the first attempt? If it's the same failure each
time, the retries are papering over a systematic defect — the pipeline
produces broken output and retries until the gates happen to pass. If
it's different failures, the pipeline is flaky, and flakiness doesn't
scale. Either way, "847 tests pass" at the end doesn't tell you whether
the output is correct or just eventually-gate-passing. The fix is not
"stop retrying" — retries are a recovery mechanism. The fix is "stop
calling 100% lock rate 'success' when 0% first-attempt means the pipeline
is broken." Gate on first-attempt pass rate, not lock rate. If
first-attempt is above 80%, the pipeline is producing quality output; if
it's 0%, the pipeline is broken and the retries are hiding it. The
principle: any metric that reports success without reporting how the
success was achieved is a claim to verify against its own fine print —
and the fine print is where the over-claim lives.
