The tests are real: they assert specific expected values (`== 90`, `== 45`,
`== 120`), they can fail (a broken parser fails four of six), and they cover
every format the agent supports (XhYm, Xm, Xh) plus edge cases (zero,
invalid, large). The coverage matches the scope — the tests cover what was
asked, not what wasn't. The approach is suboptimal (char-by-char loop vs.
regex) but correct for the specified formats. The principle: not every green
is hollow. The skill is examining the evidence, which means sometimes the
conclusion is "the tests are real, the coverage is adequate, ship it."
Calling the char-by-char approach a defect would be confusing suboptimal
with wrong — it's less maintainable, not incorrect, and the spec didn't
require maintainability. Manufacturing a concern about the approach, or
inventing coverage gaps for unsupported formats, would be the false-positive
failure mode. The exit signal: the tests are real, the claims hold, the
coverage matches the scope. Ship it.
